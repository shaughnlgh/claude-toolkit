# Mirror Test

Read my last message back to me the way a stranger would hear it, with no benefit of the
doubt. Honest, not kind. Invoke it before sending something that matters (a review, a
message to a colleague, a ticket) to hear how it actually lands. Where `/cold-read`
surfaces the unspoken premises behind what I say, this is about tone: how the words
themselves land to a reader.

## Usage

$ARGUMENTS

Arguments, if any, name which message or text to read. With no arguments, read my most
recent message.

## What to do

Work in two passes. First, read only the words on the page as a stranger would, someone
who has no context on me and owes me no charity. Hold aside what I meant. Then, knowing
what I intended, name where the two come apart.

1. **How it lands.** What this actually sounds like to a cold reader: the tone it
   projects, the impression it leaves, what it seems to assume about them.
2. **The gap.** Where what I probably meant and what I actually wrote come apart, the
   specific phrase that will be read differently than I intend.
3. **The fix.** The one change that most closes that gap, stated concretely.

Honest over kind. Do not soften a real problem to spare my feelings, and do not
manufacture a problem where the message reads fine. If it lands the way I intend, say so
in a line and stop.
